# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

BEACON is a technical project and its issue tracker, pull requests and discussions should
stay technical. Everyone who takes part — whatever their experience level, background,
employer or identity — is entitled to a place where they can ask a naive question, disagree
with a design decision, or point out a mistake without being made to feel unwelcome.

## Expected behaviour

* Be direct about the code and respectful towards the person. Criticise the patch, not the
  author.
* Assume the other person is acting in good faith, and ask before concluding that they are
  not.
* Explain your reasoning. "This will break when the seed changes" is useful; "this is
  wrong" is not.
* Accept that maintainers may decline a change, and that a "no" is about scope and fit
  rather than about you.
* Keep reviews specific and actionable, and say so when you are unsure.
* Be patient with people using the project for the first time. Simulation stacks fail in
  confusing ways when a dependency is missing.

## Unacceptable behaviour

* Harassment, insults, threats or personal attacks of any kind, in public or in private.
* Discriminatory language or imagery, including on the basis of race, ethnicity, nationality,
  gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, disability, age or appearance.
* Sexual attention or advances directed at anyone in the project.
* Deliberate intimidation, stalking, doxxing, or publishing someone's private information
  without permission.
* Sustained disruption of discussions: spamming, repeatedly reopening settled decisions,
  or derailing threads.
* Passing off someone else's work as your own.

## Scope

This applies to the repository (issues, pull requests, review comments, commits), to any
project space hosted elsewhere, and to public interactions where someone is representing
BEACON.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, report it privately to the maintainer.
The repository's **Security** tab has a **Report a vulnerability** form that opens a private
channel and is the most reliable way to reach the maintainer without the report being
public; a direct message to the maintainer on GitHub works too.

Include what happened, where, when and who was involved. Reports are handled
confidentially: the maintainer will not share the reporter's identity with the person
reported unless the reporter agrees. Reports made in good faith will never lead to
retaliation against the reporter.

## Enforcement

The maintainer will respond as promptly as is reasonable and will decide on an action
proportionate to the behaviour and its impact. Possible responses, in increasing order:

1. A private word explaining why the behaviour was unwelcome.
2. A public correction or an edited or removed comment.
3. A formal warning.
4. A temporary ban from interaction with the project.
5. A permanent ban.

Anyone asked to stop must stop immediately. A maintainer who is directly involved in an
incident will not decide on its outcome alone; if that leaves no uninvolved maintainer, the
decision will be made in public view in the issue tracker with the reasoning stated.
